UNIA stands for Unity New Input Accessory.

![UNIA: a mouse with a lid — photo 1](../../../assets/posts/telegram-161/01.webp)

Earlier I wrote about mice for small hands. Time to show one for large hands =)

![UNIA: a mouse with a lid — photo 2](../../../assets/posts/telegram-161/02.webp)

Meet the mouse with a lid. Underneath is a full seventeen-key numeric keypad. The target device was a Windows 3.x/95 laptop without a built-in number pad. The mouse itself isn't particularly mobile: twice the weight of an average mouse and thick enough to be awkward in an ordinary laptop bag.

![UNIA: a mouse with a lid — photo 3](../../../assets/posts/telegram-161/03.webp)

Despite having a keyboard inside, it plugs into a normal COM port and appears as both mouse and keyboard. An 80C51 microcontroller alternates mouse and keyboard signals, letting both operate through one port without extra drivers or a second laptop port.

The [original patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5457480A/en?oq=US%E2%80%AF5457480) describes a slightly different mechanism: a lid signal controlled switching, disabling the mouse while the lid was open.

![UNIA: a mouse with a lid — photo 4](../../../assets/posts/telegram-161/04.webp)

The device is so elaborate that in 1996 it entered [Bill Buxton's collection](https://www.microsoft.com/buxtoncollection/type.aspx?t=Mouse). Recommended, by the way: there aren't that many mice, but the engineering creativity across its devices won't leave you indifferent.

![UNIA: a mouse with a lid — photo 5](../../../assets/posts/telegram-161/05.webp)

Production ended in 1997 as laptops with built-in touchpads and lightweight optical mice requiring no pad began to appear.
