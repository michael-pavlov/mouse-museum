Continuing the tour of mice with design awards.

![ICA Technology Aero IM-PC20 — photo 1](../../../assets/posts/telegram-182/01.webp)

1989: a futuristic symmetrical three-button case, rather like a UFO. Some people see echoes of the Lincoln Futura and similar cars. The [1991 iF Design Award description](https://ifdesign.com/en/winner-ranking/project/aero-mouse/18425) explicitly calls it spaceship-like.

![ICA Technology Aero IM-PC20 — photo 2](../../../assets/posts/telegram-182/02.webp)

I couldn't find an electronic manual, but an original turned up in the [Russian National Public Library for Science and Technology](http://www.gpntb.ru/). After a quest involving a reader's card and getting the manual delivered to the correct branch, I finally enjoyed its seventy — seventy! — pages.

![ICA Technology Aero IM-PC20 — photo 3](../../../assets/posts/telegram-182/03.webp)

It is astonishingly detailed. Stretching mouse setup and operation over thirty pages takes some effort. A real pedant wrote this.

Calling the modes Slow, Normal and Fast instead of Slow, Normal and Turbo would have saved us guessing what the T button meant. Slow, at 40 dpi, is for drawing; Dr. Halo Plus was included, something like an early AutoCAD. Turbo, at 1,000 dpi, is for large screens. Remember, anything above 640 × 480 counted as large.

![ICA Technology Aero IM-PC20 — photo 4](../../../assets/posts/telegram-182/04.webp)

A second switch selects two protocols: MS for Microsoft compatibility, which someone even connected to Windows 10, and PC for Mouse Systems compatibility and Sun equipment. Included software could configure the buttons for left-handed use and assign actions in a selection of supposedly popular applications.

![ICA Technology Aero IM-PC20 — photo 5](../../../assets/posts/telegram-182/05.webp)

The manual also tells us the mouse was tested over 480 km, so they recommend cleaning it properly. I'll put the instructions in the comments for anyone needing a refresher.
