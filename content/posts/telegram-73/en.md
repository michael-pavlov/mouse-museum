Continuing our P&G archaeology. The year is 1984:

![Penny & Giles TrackerMouse — photo 1](../../../assets/posts/telegram-73/01.webp)

- The first wireless trackball.
- The first combined trackball and mouse.
- The first trackball designed specifically for a portable computer, the [Apricot Portable](https://www.computinghistory.org.uk/det/2143/Apricot-Portable/).

![Penny & Giles TrackerMouse — photo 2](../../../assets/posts/telegram-73/02.webp)

Versions came with different interfaces, eventually including PS/2, and with calculator or extra-keyboard front panels. Mine is neither the oldest nor the fanciest version.

![Penny & Giles TrackerMouse — photo 3](../../../assets/posts/telegram-73/03.webp)

The mechanism and sensors are very similar to other P&G mice; see the previous post. The long side buttons let you use it as a mouse, moving the whole device across the desk. There was also a third method: turn the ball between two fingers, one above and one below, for very precise positioning. Despite the concept, contemporary publications mainly sold it as a trackball.

![Penny & Giles TrackerMouse — photo 4](../../../assets/posts/telegram-73/04.webp)

Even then, the wireless version had a special compatibility mode for two trackballs in one room, something some televisions still can't boast of today.
